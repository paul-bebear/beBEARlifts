## Setup
1. **Starting Position**: Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart. Clean two kettlebells to the rack position — hold one in each hand at shoulder height, elbows tucked close to your body, and palms facing inward or slightly turned in.

## Execution
2. **Grip & Stance**: Keep your wrists straight and core engaged. Ensure your back is neutral and feet are firmly planted.
3. **Press**: Press both kettlebells overhead in a controlled motion, rotating your palms to face forward as you extend your arms. Stop just before your elbows lock out.
4. **Return**: Slowly lower the kettlebells back to the rack position with control, preparing for the next repetition.

## Form Tips
- **Elbow Path**: Keep elbows slightly forward, not flared out to the sides.
- **Neutral Spine**: Avoid leaning back. Maintain a tall, upright torso.
- **Controlled Breathing**: Exhale as you press the kettlebells up; inhale as you lower them down.
- **Shoulder Stability**: Pack your shoulders down and back throughout the movement to protect your joints.

## Common Mistakes
- **Overarching the Lower Back**: Engage your glutes and core to avoid hyperextension.
- **Pressing Too Fast**: Don’t rely on momentum — focus on slow, deliberate presses.
- **Losing Rack Position**: Make sure the kettlebells stay close to your forearms in the rack.

### Set the Rack Position
Stand with feet shoulder-width apart. Clean two kettlebells to shoulder height, elbows tucked and wrists straight.

### Press Overhead
Press both kettlebells up and rotate your palms to face forward. Stop just before your elbows lock.

### Lower to the Rack
Lower the kettlebells back to the rack position with control. Keep them close to your forearms.

### Keep Your Torso Tall
Brace your core and glutes so your lower back does not arch. Exhale on the press and inhale on the way down.
